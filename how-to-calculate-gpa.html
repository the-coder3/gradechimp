How to Calculate Your GPA (Step-by-Step)

7 min read · Updated July 2026

Your GPA (Grade Point Average) is a single number that represents your academic performance across all your classes. Whether you're applying to college, trying to get on the Dean's List, or just want to know where you stand, understanding how GPA works is essential.

There are two types of GPA: unweighted (the standard 4.0 scale) and weighted (which gives extra points for AP, Honors, and IB classes). We'll cover both in this guide, along with how to calculate your cumulative GPA across multiple semesters.

How to Calculate Unweighted GPA

An unweighted GPA treats all classes equally, regardless of difficulty. An A in PE counts the same as an A in AP Chemistry. Here's the formula:

GPA = Total Grade Points ÷ Number of Classes
📝 Example: Unweighted GPA

You're taking 5 classes this semester:

• English: A (4.0)

• Math: B+ (3.3)

• Science: A- (3.7)

• History: B (3.0)

• Spanish: A (4.0)

Step 1: Add up grade points: 4.0 + 3.3 + 3.7 + 3.0 + 4.0 = 18.0

Step 2: Divide by number of classes: 18.0 ÷ 5 = 3.6 GPA

Same grades, but now with class types:

• English (Regular): A = 4.0

• Math (AP): B+ = 4.3

• Science (Honors): A- = 4.2

• History (Regular): B = 3.0

• Spanish (Regular): A = 4.0

Weighted GPA: (4.0 + 4.3 + 4.2 + 3.0 + 4.0) ÷ 5 = 3.9 GPA

Compare that to the 3.6 unweighted GPA — the harder classes boosted it by 0.3 points.

How to Calculate GPA with Credits

In college (and some high schools), classes have different credit hours. A 4-credit class matters more than a 1-credit class. Here's how to account for that:

GPA = (Sum of Grade Points × Credits) ÷ Total Credits
📝 Example: GPA with Credits

• Calculus (4 credits): B+ = 3.3 → 3.3 × 4 = 13.2

• English (3 credits): A = 4.0 → 4.0 × 3 = 12.0

• Chemistry (4 credits): B = 3.0 → 3.0 × 4 = 12.0

• Art (2 credits): A = 4.0 → 4.0 × 2 = 8.0

• PE (1 credit): A = 4.0 → 4.0 × 1 = 4.0

Total quality points: 13.2 + 12.0 + 12.0 + 8.0 + 4.0 = 49.2

Total credits: 4 + 3 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 14

GPA: 49.2 ÷ 14 = 3.51

Notice how this is different from just averaging the grades (which would give 3.66). The credit-weighted GPA is lower because the lower grades (B+ and B) were in 4-credit classes, which carry more weight.

How to Calculate Cumulative GPA

Your cumulative GPA combines all semesters into one number. There are two ways to do this:

Method 1: If you know your previous GPA and credits

Cumulative GPA = (Old GPA × Old Credits + New GPA × New Credits) ÷ Total Credits
📝 Example

Previous: 3.2 GPA with 30 credits

This semester: 3.8 GPA with 15 credits

Cumulative: (3.2 × 30 + 3.8 × 15) ÷ (30 + 15) = (96 + 57) ÷ 45 = 3.4 GPA

Method 2: Add up everything from scratch

If you have your grades from every class you've ever taken, you can add up all the quality points and divide by total credits. This is more accurate but takes longer. Our GPA Calculator has a cumulative GPA option that handles this automatically.

7 Ways to Raise Your GPA

1. Prioritize high-credit classes. A grade improvement in a 4-credit class moves your GPA twice as much as in a 2-credit class.

2. Don't skip homework. Homework is usually easy points. Getting 100% on homework that's worth 15% of your grade is way easier than acing an exam.

3. Go to office hours. Students who visit professors' office hours score an average of half a letter grade higher. It's free tutoring.

4. Retake classes if allowed. Many schools let you retake a class and replace the old grade. Turning a D into a B can significantly boost your GPA.

5. Take classes you're good at. If you need elective credits, choose subjects where you naturally excel. An easy A helps your GPA.

6. Study smarter, not longer. Use active recall and spaced repetition instead of re-reading notes. 30 minutes of practice problems beats 2 hours of highlighting.

GPA FAQs

Does my GPA reset in college?

Yes. Your college GPA starts fresh — your high school GPA doesn't carry over. However, colleges do look at your high school GPA during admissions.

Do plus/minus grades affect GPA?

At most schools, yes. An A- (3.7) is lower than an A (4.0), and a B+ (3.3) is higher than a B (3.0). Some schools don't use plus/minus — check your school's grading policy.

Is a 3.5 GPA good?

Yes. A 3.5 GPA puts you above average and qualifies you for most scholarships and academic honors. It's roughly equivalent to an A-/B+ average.

How much does one bad grade hurt my GPA?

It depends on how many total credits you have. Early in your academic career, one bad grade has a big impact. After you've accumulated 60+ credits, one class barely moves the needle. Use our GPA Calculator with the cumulative option to see the exact impact.

Can I calculate my GPA without credit hours?

Yes — just use the simple method (total grade points ÷ number of classes). This gives you an unweighted average that's a close approximation for most high school students.
